# Six scenes, one week

**Purpose:** the method is written as rules, but rules are learned at the moment they are needed. This is the tutorial: six situations you will actually meet in the first week, each one small enough to read in a minute, each one with the reflex it replaces.

**How to use it:** read one card before the day it belongs to. Do not read all six at once — that is the same mistake as reading the method on Sunday and expecting it to stick. The video is the same six scenes, watched once.

**The one line behind all six:** the **Kick-off grid** is what they said today. The **OPEN sheet** is what they owe, and by when. The **Notebook** is what happened. Nothing else holds state.

---

## 1 · Monday 06:55 — the week is already printed

**Situation.** Coffee, desk, and the old reflex arrives: build the week. A blank page, eleven names to write, dates to work out, and by the time it is ready the board has started.

**What you do.**

1. Take the pack printed on Sunday: one **OPEN sheet**, five **Kick-off grids**, already dated, names already printed.
2. Write the week's **3 targets** at the top of the **OPEN sheet**, each with a date.
3. Write the fixed points into `TODAY'S 3` on the right day — the TBT on Tuesday, SHI on Thursday, Thierry Friday 14:00.
4. Stop at ten minutes.

**What you do not do.** Copy last week's page line by line. Write the eleven names again. Build a separate calendar for the week's appointments.

**Where it lands.** Targets → **OPEN sheet**. Appointments → `TODAY'S 3`. Nothing → the app.

**Why.** The only decision on Monday morning is *what are the three outcomes*. The paperwork was decided on Sunday.

---

## 2 · 07:20 — "PTW, still waiting"

**Situation.** The board ends. Khang says the PTW for the FIR room is still not issued, and he has no date. You have thirty seconds before the walkdown starts.

**What you do.**

1. On today's **Kick-off grid**, beside Khang, one line: `⇒ Khang: PTW FIR room — date TBC`.
2. Before the walkdown, one row on the **OPEN sheet**: what, WHO `Khang`, and a DUE date. You set the date, not him — `26.11`.
3. If the room is on the power-up path, add `!` and circle the date.

**What you do not do.** Leave the line on the grid and hope to remember it. Write "PTW issue" with no name. Wait for a date to arrive by itself.

**Where it lands.** The **Kick-off grid** keeps what he said today. The **OPEN sheet** keeps what he owes, and by when.

**Why.** A line with no date on the **Kick-off grid** is a memory. The same line with a date on the **OPEN sheet** is a follow-up. The grid is the doorway; the OPEN sheet is the room.

---

## 3 · 10:40 — the idea in the corridor

**Situation.** Walking back from the ECC room you think: a 5S campaign in there would end the cable mess. Nobody owes anything. There is no date. It is not a commitment, and it is too good to lose.

**What you do.**

1. Back at the desk, on the back page of the **Notebook**: `24.11 IDEA 5S campaign, ECC room`.
2. Nothing else. It does not touch the **OPEN sheet**.
3. On **Friday 13:00** it gets one of three fates: struck out, turned into `→ me: draft 5S plan for ECC — 09.12` on the **OPEN sheet**, or moved to `ROADMAP.md` if it is about the system rather than the ship.

**What you do not do.** Start a second list for ideas. Put an undated idea in the DUE column. Feel obliged to act on it this week.

**Where it lands.** The back of the **Notebook**, until Friday decides.

**Why.** The **OPEN sheet** only holds things someone owes. An idea sitting in that column is a lie with a date on it — and the first time one slips, you stop trusting the page.

---

## 4 · 13:10 — the target that moves

**Situation.** Target 3 is *AIR LWN ready & signed, 26.11*. At 13:10 the power-up is cancelled: conditions not met. The week's third outcome is no longer true.

**What you do.**

1. Strike through target 3. Do not erase it, do not reword it quietly.
2. Write the new outcome beside it, with the date and the reason: `3 AIR LWN readiness check · 23.11 pm, power-up off (conditions)`.
3. One `•` line on the **Notebook** page: the decision, who took it, when.
4. Update the **OPEN sheet** rows that depended on the power-up date.

**What you do not do.** Rewrite the target cleanly so the change disappears. Leave the old date in the DUE column. Explain it on Friday from memory.

**Where it lands.** The **OPEN sheet** keeps both versions, struck and new. The **Notebook** keeps the decision.

**Why.** On Friday the report can say *target changed on the 23rd, power-up cancelled on conditions* without you reconstructing anything. The strikethrough is the audit trail.

---

## 5 · 16:30 — three lines, not an essay

**Situation.** End of the day. Eight things happened, none of them are on paper, and the temptation is to write the day down properly.

**What you do.**

1. On today's **Notebook** page, three `•` lines: `done`, `moved`, `blocked`. One line each.
2. Tomorrow's appointments into tomorrow's `TODAY'S 3`.
3. Send the nudges for everything due today, before the laptop closes.

**What you do not do.** Retell the day. Write a page. Leave the nudges for tomorrow morning.

**Where it lands.** The **Notebook** holds the day's record. The **OPEN sheet** holds what is still owed.

**Why.** The three lines are the raw material for Friday. If Tuesday is not a line, Friday is fiction.

---

## 6 · Friday 13:00 — the report writes itself

**Situation.** 13:00. Thierry at 14:00. The old panic: what did we actually achieve this week?

**What you do.**

1. **OPEN sheet:** tick `✓` what closed, mark `✗` what did not, read the struck-through targets.
2. **Notebook:** read the week's `•` and `!` lines. Write three lines: done / blocked / risk.
3. Assemble the report from those lines, in Thierry's fixed format. Fifteen minutes.
4. Close the laptop. 14:00 is now a meeting, not a reconstruction.

**What you do not do.** Rely on memory. Invent a new report format. Carry open rows in silence — every open row either gets a new date or becomes next week's line.

**Where it lands.** The weekly report, generated from the **OPEN sheet** and the **Notebook**, not from recall.

**Why.** If Friday is hard, the failure happened on Tuesday at 16:30, not on Friday.

---

### If only one thing survives

> The **Kick-off grid** is what they said. The **OPEN sheet** is what they owe. The **Notebook** is what happened.

The **OPEN sheet** is the only page you read. If it is true on Friday, the week was captured. If it is not, no report format will save it.
