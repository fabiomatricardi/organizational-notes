The six marks

MarkWrite it whenShape of the line
•something happened or was decidedwhat + so what

Plus @name at the start when the line is about a person (a commitment, an ask, a personal note).

Test for a good line: a stranger reading it next Friday knows who does what by when. If not, rewrite it in five more seconds.

Three things this board proves, and the reason the sheet exists:

  1. The board duplicates across areas. LUN schedule, BICC check and W48 TPC OWS appear in two columns each. The register holds one line with an area tag — the duplicate becomes a decision instead of a twice-maintained fact.
  2. One item, three names. LUN has Aras/Chang in FIR and Aras/Seong/Anto in AIR. The board can live with that; a commitment cannot. One owner, the others named as support.
  3. Partial completion is invisible on a board. The keys are marked 0-T? in LQ, FIR and AIR with no distinction between "asked", "promised" and "received". On the register: ? keys (0-T): LQ ✓ / FIR ? / AIR ? — you can see at a glance which room you are still blocked on.

The 60-second transfer, every morning

  1. Photo the board. Do not transcribe it.
  2. On the kick-off grid, one line per person — only what they said that is new or that they owe.
  3. After the meeting, move only ⇒ / → / ? / ! onto the A4 register. The board's area blocks are status, not commitments; they belong in the day's LOG entry, not in the register.

Anti-patterns (each one is a future ghost)

  • No outcome. "Meeting with Emerson about DCS." → What was decided? Who does what by when?
  • No date. "Chase BICC." → By when? You set the dates; that is your job.
  • Two owners. "Aras and Buly to sort the loop folders." → Two lines, two dates.
  • The essay. Eight lines about one walkdown. → One line, plus the punch items as ⇒.
  • The pronoun. "He will check." → Name.
  • The misc. "Misc issues with ICSS." → Not actionable; either name the issue or leave it off.
  • The backfill. Reconstructing Monday on Wednesday. → gap 17-18.11 and move on.
